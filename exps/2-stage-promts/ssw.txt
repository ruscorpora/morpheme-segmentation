Your task is to perform high-granularity, synchronic, surface-level morpheme segmentation of a given word.

You must segment the word into its smallest meaningful functional units based on the following linguistic principles derived from the language's parsing dictionary:

### Segmentation Principles & Granularity

1.  **Verbal Morphology (High Granularity)**:
    *   **Roots**: Isolate the verb root (e.g., `hat` in `hatisitsweng`, `sup` in `supang`).
    *   **Extensions**: Separately segment verbal extensions (derivational suffixes). Common forms include:
        *   Causative: `-is-`, `-ts-`
        *   Applicative: `-el-`, `-ets-`
        *   Reciprocal: `-an-`
        *   Passive: `-w-`, `-uw-`
    *   **Tense/Aspect**: Segment perfective markers (e.g., `-il-`, `-its-`).
    *   **Final Vowels**: The final vowel of the verb (usually `-a` or `-e`) must be segmented as a distinct unit (e.g., `sup:X/a:X/ng:X`).
    *   **Reflexives**: Isolate the reflexive prefix `i-` (e.g., `i:X/pil:X/ets:X/a:X`).

2.  **Nominal Morphology**:
    *   **Class Prefixes**: Segment noun class prefixes from the stem. Common prefixes include `mo-`, `ba-`, `le-`, `ma-`, `se-`, `di-`, `bo-`. (e.g., `di:X/trophy:X`, `ma:X/tlalo:X`, `se:X/fate:X`).
    *   **Locative/Relative Suffix**: The suffix `-ng` must be segmented separately (e.g., `Kopano:X/ng:X`, `sup:X/a:X/ng:X`).

3.  **Orthographic Adherence**:
    *   **Surface Constraint**: The segmentation is purely surface-level. Do not restore underlying phonetic forms. If a nasal is assimilated in the orthography (e.g., `m` instead of `mo` before `b`), strictly segment the characters present in the source string (e.g., `mmila` -> `m:X/mila:X`).
    *   **Concatenation**: The sum of the segmented text parts must equate exactly to the source word string.

### Rules
1.  **Tagging**: Assign the tag `X` to every morpheme.
2.  **Output Format**: Return ONLY a valid JSON object. No markdown, no preambles.
